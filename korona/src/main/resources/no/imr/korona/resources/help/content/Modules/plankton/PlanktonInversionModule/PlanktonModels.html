Plankton models

There are five plankton models implemented in the Plankton inversion module.

FluidBentCylinder

This models the backscattering of a fluid bent cylinder, taken from Stanton 1994.

The model has three configurable parameters:

  • Reflection coefficient
  • Relative standard deviation of length - std dev/L
  • Length to width ratio

The bend of the cylinder is not a parameter, as the average echo is independent of the curvature when the distribution of orientations is sufficiently broad.

Bio volume

V = L * π * r2
where L is the length of the cylinder and r is the radius.

FluidProlateSpheroid

This models the backscattering of a prolate spheroid filled with a fluid, taken from Stanton 1989.

The model has three configurable parameters:

  • g - density body material/density surrounding fluid
  • h - compressional speed of sound in body material/compressional speed of sound in surrounding fluid
  • beta - Length to width ratio.

Bio volume

V = π /6 * L * d2
where L is the length (major diameter) and d is the minor diameter of the prolate spheroid.

GaseousSphere

This models the backscattering of a gas filled sphere, taken from Stanton 1989.

The model has two parameters:

  • g - density body material/density surrounding fluid
  • h - compressional speed of sound in body material/compressional speed of sound in surrounding fluid

Bio volume

V = π /6 * L3
where L is the diameter of the sphere.

HardShelledSphere

This models the backscattering of a hard shelled sphere, taken from Stanton 1989.

The model has one parameter:

  • R - The reflection factor.

Bio volume

V = π /6 * L3
where L is the diameter of the sphere.

SDWBA

This models the backscattering of a reference size krill individual, using a parametrization of the Stochastic Distorted Wave Born Approximation (SDWBA), taken from Conti 2006.

The model takes one parameter.

  • SDWBA coeff set - The parametrization of the SDWBA is given in terms of the distribution of krill orientations N(theta, sigma), where theta is the mean krill orientation, and sigma is the standard deviation of the distribution of orientations. Coefficients for the distributions presented in the paper above are provided in this implementation.

Bio volume

V = V0 * (L/L0)3
where V0 is the volume of the reference krill, L is the length and L0 is the length of the reference krill.
