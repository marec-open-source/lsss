Working with regions

A region is a common term for layers and schools. The purpose of regions is to separate the survey into parts that can be more easily interpreted. The regions are common for all frequencies. A fundamental concept is that all region edits affect only the visible ping range. That is, the effects of region edits do not extend beyond the left or right edges of the visible part of the echogram. If the echogram is zoomed vertically, however, the effects of region edits can extend above or below the visible part of the echogram.

When interpreting a large survey, a typical use of the region system can be as follows:

  1. When viewing the whole survey, insert some 'global' horizontal layer boundaries to separate the main layers in the survey. These boundaries will then exist as a starting point when interpreting smaller segments of the survey in more detail.
  2. Add some acoustic categories to be interpreted in each of the layers, and optionally assign one or more of the acoustic categories to be rest-acoustic-category (see interpretation module).
  3. Optionally exclude some ping ranges from further interpretation. Typically, a user may want to exclude parts of a survey where the boat made a detour to take trawl samples.
  4. Start a more detailed interpretation by zooming in, or by clicking the button for the preferred segment size on the toolbar.
  5. Add more layers and school regions as needed, and assign interpretations to the regions.
